# Cell: The Unit of Life

## Cell theory
Cell theory states that all living organisms are made of cells, and the cell is the basic unit of structure and function. Matthias Schleiden (1838) proposed that plants are made of cells, and Theodor Schwann (1839) extended this to animals. Rudolf Virchow (1855) added that all cells arise from pre-existing cells, written as "omnis cellula e cellula".

## Prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells
Prokaryotic cells, such as bacteria, have no membrane-bound nucleus. Their genetic material is a single circular DNA lying in the cytoplasm in a region called the nucleoid. They also lack membrane-bound organelles such as mitochondria. Eukaryotic cells have a true nucleus enclosed by a nuclear envelope and contain membrane-bound organelles.

## Mitochondria
Mitochondria are double-membrane organelles known as the powerhouse of the cell because they are the site of aerobic respiration and produce ATP. The inner membrane folds into cristae, which increase the surface area for the electron transport chain. The matrix contains its own circular DNA, 70S ribosomes and the enzymes of the Krebs cycle, which is why mitochondria are called semi-autonomous organelles.

## Ribosomes
Ribosomes are non-membrane-bound organelles that synthesise proteins. Prokaryotes have 70S ribosomes made of 50S and 30S subunits, while the eukaryotic cytoplasm has 80S ribosomes made of 60S and 40S subunits. The "S" stands for Svedberg unit, a measure of sedimentation rate, so subunit values do not add up directly.

## Lysosomes
Lysosomes are single-membrane vesicles formed from the Golgi apparatus. They are rich in hydrolytic enzymes such as lipases, proteases and carbohydrases that work best at an acidic pH. Because they digest worn-out organelles and foreign material, lysosomes are called suicidal bags.

## Cell division: mitosis
Mitosis is equational division, so the two daughter cells have the same chromosome number as the parent cell. Its stages are prophase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase. In metaphase the chromosomes line up at the equatorial plate, and in anaphase the sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles.
