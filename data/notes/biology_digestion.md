# Digestion and Absorption

## Digestion in the mouth
Digestion starts in the buccal cavity. Saliva contains salivary amylase (ptyalin), which converts about 30% of starch into maltose at a pH of about 6.8. Saliva also contains lysozyme, which acts as an antibacterial agent.

## Digestion in the stomach
The gastric glands contain chief (peptic) cells, which secrete pepsinogen, and parietal (oxyntic) cells, which secrete hydrochloric acid and Castle's intrinsic factor. HCl gives a pH of about 1.8 and converts inactive pepsinogen into active pepsin, which breaks proteins into proteoses and peptones. Intrinsic factor is needed to absorb vitamin B12.

## Digestion in the small intestine
Pancreatic juice contains trypsinogen, chymotrypsinogen, procarboxypeptidases, pancreatic amylase, lipase and nucleases. Enterokinase, secreted by the intestinal mucosa, activates trypsinogen into trypsin. Bile from the liver contains no enzymes but emulsifies fats into small micelles, which helps lipase act on them.

## Absorption
Most digestion and absorption take place in the small intestine, whose villi and microvilli give a large surface area. Glucose and amino acids are absorbed into the blood, partly by active transport. Fatty acids and glycerol form micelles, are re-formed into chylomicrons inside the intestinal cells, and pass into the lacteals of the lymph system.

## Hormones of digestion
Gastrin stimulates the secretion of gastric juice. Secretin stimulates the pancreas to release water and bicarbonate ions, and cholecystokinin (CCK) stimulates the release of pancreatic enzymes and the contraction of the gall bladder.

## Disorders
Jaundice is a disorder of the liver in which bile pigments are deposited, turning the skin and eyes yellow. Diarrhoea is abnormally frequent bowel movement with more liquid faeces, reducing the absorption of food.
