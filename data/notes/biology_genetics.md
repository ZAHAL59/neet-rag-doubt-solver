# Principles of Inheritance

## Mendel's laws
Gregor Mendel worked on the garden pea, Pisum sativum, and studied seven pairs of contrasting traits. His law of dominance states that in a heterozygote only the dominant allele is expressed. His law of segregation states that the two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation, so each gamete carries only one allele. The law of independent assortment states that alleles of different genes assort independently when the genes are on different chromosomes.

## Monohybrid and dihybrid ratios
A monohybrid cross between two heterozygotes (Tt x Tt) gives a phenotypic ratio of 3:1 and a genotypic ratio of 1:2:1 in the F2 generation. A dihybrid cross between two double heterozygotes gives a phenotypic ratio of 9:3:3:1 in the F2 generation.

## Test cross
A test cross crosses an organism showing the dominant phenotype with a homozygous recessive individual. If all offspring show the dominant trait, the parent was homozygous dominant; a 1:1 ratio shows the parent was heterozygous.

## Incomplete dominance and co-dominance
In incomplete dominance the heterozygote shows an intermediate phenotype, as in snapdragon (Antirrhinum) flower colour, where red x white gives pink, and the F2 phenotypic ratio becomes 1:2:1. In co-dominance both alleles are fully expressed, as in the ABO blood group, where alleles IA and IB are co-dominant and give blood group AB.

## Sex determination in humans
Humans show XX-XY sex determination. Females are XX and males are XY. Because the male produces two kinds of gametes, with X or Y, the sex of the child is decided by the sperm.

## Genetic disorders
Haemophilia and colour blindness are X-linked recessive disorders, so they appear more often in males. Sickle-cell anaemia is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by a substitution of glutamic acid by valine at the sixth position of the beta-globin chain. Down's syndrome is caused by trisomy of chromosome 21.
