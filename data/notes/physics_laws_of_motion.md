# Laws of Motion

## Newton's first law
Newton's first law, the law of inertia, states that a body stays at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line unless an external net force acts on it. Inertia is the tendency of a body to resist a change in its state of motion, and mass is the measure of inertia.

## Newton's second law
Newton's second law states that the rate of change of momentum of a body equals the net external force on it and acts in the direction of the force. For constant mass this becomes F = ma. The SI unit of force is the newton, where 1 N = 1 kg m/s².

## Impulse
Impulse is the product of force and the time for which it acts, and it equals the change in momentum: J = F × Δt = Δp. A cricketer pulls the hands back while catching a ball to increase the time of contact, which reduces the force on the hands for the same change in momentum.

## Newton's third law
Newton's third law states that to every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. Action and reaction act on different bodies, so they never cancel each other. Rocket propulsion and the recoil of a gun are examples.

## Conservation of momentum
If no external force acts on a system, its total linear momentum stays constant. When a gun of mass M fires a bullet of mass m with velocity v, the gun recoils with velocity V = -mv/M.

## Friction
Static friction acts when there is no relative motion and can adjust up to a maximum value, called limiting friction, f = μs N. Kinetic friction acts once motion starts and is usually smaller than limiting friction, so μk < μs. Rolling friction is smaller than sliding friction.

## Motion on a banked road
On a banked road the maximum safe speed is v_max = √[rg (μs + tanθ)/(1 − μs tanθ)]. Without friction, the optimum speed at which no wear occurs on the tyres is v_o = √(rg tanθ).
