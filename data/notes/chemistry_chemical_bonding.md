# Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure

## VSEPR theory
VSEPR theory predicts molecular shape from the repulsion between electron pairs in the valence shell of the central atom. The order of repulsion is lone pair–lone pair > lone pair–bond pair > bond pair–bond pair. Because lone pairs repel more strongly, they reduce bond angles.

## Shapes and bond angles
Methane (CH4) has four bond pairs and is tetrahedral with a bond angle of 109.5°. Ammonia (NH3) has three bond pairs and one lone pair, giving a trigonal pyramidal shape with a bond angle of about 107°. Water (H2O) has two bond pairs and two lone pairs, giving a bent shape with a bond angle of about 104.5°.

## Hybridisation
In sp3 hybridisation one s and three p orbitals mix to form four equivalent orbitals at 109.5°, as in methane. sp2 hybridisation gives three orbitals at 120° in a trigonal planar arrangement, as in BF3 and ethene. sp hybridisation gives two orbitals at 180°, a linear arrangement, as in BeCl2 and ethyne. In PCl5 phosphorus is sp3d hybridised and trigonal bipyramidal, and in SF6 sulphur is sp3d2 hybridised and octahedral.

## Bond order
In molecular orbital theory, bond order = ½ (Nb − Na), where Nb and Na are the numbers of electrons in bonding and antibonding orbitals. The O2 molecule has a bond order of 2 and is paramagnetic because it has two unpaired electrons in its antibonding π* orbitals. N2 has a bond order of 3, which explains its very high bond strength. A higher bond order means a shorter bond and a higher bond dissociation energy.

## Hydrogen bonding
A hydrogen bond forms when hydrogen is bonded to a highly electronegative atom such as F, O or N and is attracted to another such atom. Intermolecular hydrogen bonding raises boiling points, which is why water has a much higher boiling point than H2S. Intramolecular hydrogen bonding, as in o-nitrophenol, lowers the boiling point compared to the para isomer.

## Dipole moment
Dipole moment is the product of charge and the distance between the charges, measured in debye. CO2 and BF3 have zero dipole moment because their symmetrical shapes cancel the bond dipoles, while NH3 and H2O are polar molecules.
